01

About

I work on data that arrive many times a day and then keep arriving for years: mood reported four times daily for a week, a diary kept each night, a cohort of adolescents measured again every spring. Data like this carry two questions at once — where is this person heading over the long run, and what governs them over the course of a day. Most available methods answer one of the two and quietly absorb the other into the residuals, which is how a developmental trend ends up masquerading as a strong autoregressive effect.

My dissertation is about measurement burst designs — short, dense bursts of observation separated by long gaps — and models that do them justice. BurstiVAR estimates within-person vector autoregressive dynamics while free person- and burst-specific intercepts absorb the across-burst trend, so nothing has to be assumed about its functional form. Bursti-CTVAR moves that idea into continuous time, where the long gaps between bursts and the irregular spacing inside them become part of the model rather than a nuisance for the analyst. Both are Bayesian and multilevel, written for Stan and JAGS, and released with the simulation code and runnable tutorials behind them.

Applied collaboration keeps the methods honest. I have worked on bullying victimisation and depressive symptoms among middle-school students, on what distinguishes adolescents who attempt suicide from those who do not, on daily meaning and relationship quality in an eight-week intervention trial, and on a multi-laboratory effort to predict affect from physiology whose most useful result was a clear account of where machine learning fell short. Those projects set my standard for methods work: a model is worth publishing when it changes what a substantive researcher is entitled to conclude.

Psychohistory only ever worked in the aggregate, and only across centuries. The real version runs on much less — a few weeks of one person's days — and has to stay honest about what it cannot see.

02

Research

Four problems recur in my work. Each one started as an obstacle in somebody else's analysis and turned into a methodological paper.

Trend and dynamics, estimated together BurstiVAR

A multilevel VAR model for burst designs in which autoregressive and cross-lagged parameters describe the within-person process instead of absorbing the developmental slope. Current work maps where the approximation holds and where it fails.

Continuous time, unequal spacing Bursti-CTVAR

A Bayesian multilevel, multi-timescale continuous-time VAR model for multiple bursts, built so that long between-burst gaps and irregular within-burst schedules are features of the design rather than problems to be smoothed away.

Abrupt change or gradual drift? Model selection

Entropy and model-fit measures that distinguish a genuine structural break from slow drift in Bayesian longitudinal models — and that say when the two are not distinguishable at all.

Reliability when the target is a process Measurement

Classical reliability was built for a score, not for an inertia or a cross-lagged effect. I study what precision means for individual-level dynamic parameters in multilevel continuous-time models, and what a design has to deliver before those parameters can be trusted.

04

Software

Every model I publish ships with the code that produced the results in the paper, including the simulation studies, not only a worked example.

BurstiVAR

R · JAGS · MIT licence

Multilevel VAR for measurement burst designs, together with GoBurstiVAR, a nested parametric variant that replaces the free burst intercepts with a Gompertz growth function. The repository holds the full parameter-recovery and efficiency simulations, the empirical analysis, the convergence diagnostics behind the appendix tables, the figure scripts, and two runnable tutorials — the BurstiVAR one finishes in about a minute.

Repository What it does

mlGAR

R · JAGS

Multilevel growth with autoregressive residuals: a Gompertz trend and a multilevel AR(1) process estimated together in one Bayesian model, rather than detrending first and modelling the residuals afterwards. It recovers parameters with as few as five time points per person, where two-stage approaches break down. Includes a tutorial vignette covering data generation, model specification, sampling and diagnostics.
